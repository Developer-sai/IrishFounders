# Irish Founders — Full-Stack Platform Prototype

The concept should be built as a verified, invitation-by-approval startup ecosystem, not another open-registration social network.

Irish Founders will be a free platform connecting genuine founders, startups, investors, and ecosystem partners across Ireland. Every account goes through verification and a short interview before receiving access to the community.

The most important principle is:

Real founders. Real companies. Real investors. No fake profiles, duplicate companies, or fabricated engagement.

I'll structure the build prompt for Lovable around a complete product, including the public website, founder experience, investor experience, verification and onboarding, community features, and a dedicated super-admin dashboard.

Yes. You can legally create and operate Irish Founders in Ireland, including a curated, members-only community for verified startup founders, investors, and entrepreneurs.

There is no general Irish law prohibiting someone from establishing an independent startup community, creating a founder networking platform, reviewing membership applications, or requiring applicants to complete an interview.

You also do not ordinarily need permission from Enterprise Ireland, NDRC, Dogpatch Labs, or another startup organisation to establish your own independent community.

However, there are several legal requirements worth understanding before launching `irishfounders.ie`, particularly because your platform will verify identities, collect LinkedIn information, host user-generated content, and connect founders with investors.

The distinction is that building a founder networking platform is generally straightforward legally; operating it responsibly requires some compliance work.

## 1. What is legally allowed?

For your proposed platform:

| Platform feature              | General legal position                                              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Free founder community        | Allowed                                                             |
| Invite-only membership        | Allowed                                                             |
| Manual founder interviews     | Allowed, subject to privacy and fair procedures                     |
| Minimum 50 LinkedIn followers | Generally allowed as an admission criterion                         |
| Requiring 2–3 company posts   | Generally allowed                                                   |
| Verified founder badges       | Allowed if truthful and not misleading                              |
| Public startup directory      | Allowed, subject to privacy and other rights                        |
| Private founder messaging     | Allowed, with applicable compliance                                 |

You can set objective membership standards for your private community. Make those standards transparent, consistently applied, and non-discriminatory.

## 2. The six legal areas you should address

### A. Business name and domain

You can propose the name Irish Founders, but you should first check whether anyone has conflicting rights to it.

Check these three sources:

- [Companies Registration Office (CRO)](https://core.cro.ie/) — existing companies and business names.
- [Intellectual Property Office of Ireland](https://www.ipoi.gov.ie/) — registered trademarks.
- [.IE domain registry](https://www.weare.ie/) — domain availability.

If you carry on business in Ireland under Irish Founders rather than your own legal name, business-name registration will generally be required.

According to the CRO, registering a business name does not itself provide trademark protection.&#x20;

### B. GDPR — particularly important

Your application will collect:

- Founder names and emails.
- LinkedIn profile URLs.
- Interview responses.
- Startup affiliations.
- Investor information.
- Potentially identity verification documents.

Because you are processing personal information, GDPR applies even when your community is completely free.

You should have a privacy policy explaining why information is collected, its lawful basis, who can access it, retention periods, and members' data-protection rights.&#x20;

### C. Digital Services Act (DSA)

This is particularly relevant because Irish Founders will host user-generated content.

Your community feed, startup profiles, product launches, and public discussions may bring the platform within the EU Digital Services Act.

The exact obligations depend on how the service is classified and its size.

Plan for:

- Content reporting.

- Illegal-content reporting and handling.
- Moderation decisions with explanations.

### E. LinkedIn verification

Your proposed requirement of 50+ followers and 2–3 posts is generally a platform admission rule, not inherently a legal problem.

However, avoid unauthorised scraping, automated extraction, or representing LinkedIn as an official verification partner.

For the MVP, founders can submit their company LinkedIn URL and post links, which administrators manually review.

### F. Immigration and business activity

One additional consideration: if you are living in Ireland on immigration permission, check whether that permission allows you to establish and actively operate a business.

For example, Stamp 2 student permission generally restricts self-employment and business activity.

This is separate from whether the Irish Founders platform itself is lawful.

Before formally operating it, check your specific permission with the Irish Immigration Service or a qualified immigration adviser.

## 4. Do you need to register a company immediately?

There is a difference between developing a prototype and operating a business.

| Stage                                    | Suggested approach                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Designing in Lovable                     | Build the prototype                                                        |
| Testing with a small internal group      | Use controlled test accounts and appropriate privacy safeguards            |
| Opening public applications              | Establish the responsible legal operator and publish the required policies |
| Running a public community               | Complete applicable business, data protection, and platform compliance     |
| Introducing regulated financial services | Obtain specialist legal advice before proceeding                           |

An Irish LTD company is one possible structure, but incorporation is not automatically mandatory merely because you are developing a website. Your trading activities, liability exposure, immigration permission, and other circumstances matter.

The CRO explains the available company formation process here: [Registering an Irish company](https://cro.ie/registration/company/required-steps/).&#x20;

## Final assessment

Yes — Irish Founders is a legally viable concept in Ireland. Nothing about your proposed free, curated founder community inherently requires you to obtain permission from an existing startup organisation.

I would prioritise three things before inviting the first real members: checking the Irish Founders name, ensuring your own permission allows you to operate the venture, and putting GDPR-compliant verification and membership processes in place.

You can then launch a focused founder community and add investor networking without initially entering regulated investment activities.

A short pre-launch consultation with an Irish solicitor familiar with technology platforms, GDPR, and financial-services boundaries would be worthwhile.
